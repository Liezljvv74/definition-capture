# The problem

- Grammar questions come up constantly, and there is rarely a teacher to ask
- Forums and search results are slow, generic, or pitched at the wrong level
- Answers get lost in a chat window or a pile of notebooks
- Words, phrases and grammar notes end up scattered across apps and paper

<!--
Anyone who has learned a language knows this moment: you're reading or writing, and a grammar question comes up. Why is this word in this form? When do I use this tense?

Most learners don't have a teacher on hand. So they post on a forum and wait, or search and get an answer that's too advanced, too basic, or just not about their question.

And even when they do get a good answer, it disappears. It's in a chat history, a screenshot, or a notebook they'll never open again.

On top of that, their vocabulary lives in one app, their notes in another, and their grammar in a third. Nothing connects.
-->

# How it works

<div class="grid grid-cols-3 gap-8 mt-12 text-center">
<div class="card sky tape" style="--r:-1deg">

## 1. Ask

A grammar question, in plain words

</div>
<div class="card sage tape" style="--r:1.5deg">

## 2. Keep

The answer, saved as a grammar rule

</div>
<div class="card blue tape" style="--r:-0.5deg">

## 3. Review

With your words and phrases, as flashcards

</div>
</div>

<!--
The idea is simple and fits in three steps.

First, you ask. You type a grammar question in plain words, the way you'd ask a teacher.

Second, you keep. When an answer is useful, you save it as a grammar rule in your notebook, linked to the words and phrases it's about.

Third, you review. Your words and phrases come back as flashcards when they're due, so what you've collected actually sticks.

The rest of the app is built around those three steps.
-->

<img src="/tutor-answer.png" class="shot" alt="The Tutor page answering a question about German adjectives" />

<!--
This is the heart of the app: the grammar tutor. What you see on the right is a real answer from the live site.

You ask a question and get an explanation pitched at your level, with examples.

It's a conversation, not a one-off answer, so you can follow up with "what about this case?" and it knows what you were talking about.

Every conversation is saved and listed on the side, and you can search them later, even if you don't remember the exact words.

And when an answer is worth keeping, one click saves it as a grammar rule. You can also combine several answers into one rule.

This is what makes Captured more than a vocabulary list. Without the tutor, it would be a spreadsheet. With it, learners build their own grammar reference out of their own questions.
-->

# One notebook for everything you learn

<div class="grid grid-cols-2 gap-10 mt-4 items-center">
<div>

- **Vocabulary**: words, definitions, and where you found them
- **Phrases**: expressions, their literal meaning, and an example
- **Verb tables**: a conjugation table for every verb
- **Grammar rules**: explanations with tables and examples, by topic

Any item can link to any other, so a rule leads to the words it explains.

</div>
<img src="/notes-cards.png" class="shot w-[88%]" style="--r:1.5deg" alt="The four kinds of notes as pastel cards: Words, Phrases, Verb tables and Grammar rules" />
</div>

<!--
Alongside the tutor, Captured is a notebook with four kinds of notes.

Vocabulary: words, what they mean, and where the learner came across them, grouped into collections.

Phrases: expressions, with what they literally mean and an example of how they're used.

Verb tables: a full conjugation table for each verb.

And grammar rules: explanations with tables and examples, filed by topic. Many of these come straight from the tutor.

The important part is that they connect. A grammar rule can link to the words and phrases it's about, so everything a learner knows about a topic is one click apart.
-->

# Practice that sticks

<div class="grid grid-cols-2 gap-10 mt-4 items-center">
<div>

- **Flashcards** from any list, or only what is due today
- **Verb practice**: fill in blank conjugation tables, tense by tense
- **Read aloud**: hear any word, phrase or rule in the right voice
- **A home page** showing what is due and how far you have come

</div>
<img src="/flashcard.png" class="shot" style="--r:-1.5deg" alt="A flashcard for la mariposa, with a box to type the meaning and a Check button" />
</div>

<!--
Collecting notes is only half the job. You also need to remember them.

Flashcards bring words and phrases back when they're due. You type the answer, it's marked, and the schedule adjusts to how well you knew it.

Verb practice gives you blank tables to fill in for the tenses you choose, and each tense has its own review schedule. A verb counts as learned only when you know all of its tenses.

Read aloud lets you hear any word, phrase or whole rule, each part in the voice of its own language.

And the home page shows at a glance what's due today and the progress you've made.
-->

# Private by design

<div class="grid grid-cols-2 gap-10 mt-4 items-center">
<div>

- Every learner has their own account, and sees only their own notes
- Sign-in is protected against bots
- A dedicated test tried to reach one account's data from another
- Notes are stored safely online, never only in one browser

</div>
<img src="/private.png" class="shot" style="--r:1deg" alt="The landing page card: Your work stays private, unless you decide to share it" />
</div>

<!--
Learners trust us with their notes and their questions, so privacy was built in from the start, not added later.

Every learner has their own account, and every note belongs to that account. Nobody else can see it.

Sign-in is protected against automated bots.

We didn't just assume it works. We built a test where three accounts tried to read, change, or search each other's data in every way we could think of. All 626 checks passed: no account could reach another's notes or tutor conversations.

And because notes are stored online rather than in the browser, nothing is lost when someone switches device.
-->
